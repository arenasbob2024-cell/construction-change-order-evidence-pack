    How the checksum works

    One hash for one file.

    The tool computes SHA-256 over the finished PDF bytes. If a later copy has the same SHA-256, its bytes match the exported file. If even one byte changes, the hash changes and the check fails.

    The manifest hashes the PDF only. It does not hash a zip archive, because no zip is produced. It also does not hash your original photos: those were re-encoded before being embedded, so the PDF is the file being tracked.

    Important boundary

    This tool organizes records and computes file checksums in your browser. It does not prove what happened, when a photo was taken, who signed it, or legal admissibility. No trusted timestamp or legal certification is provided.

    Who it is for

    Small crews with scattered change records.

    A change order often lives in a text thread, an email, a paper note and a phone album. When the client questions the extra work, the office has to reconstruct the story. This page turns one change order into a single PDF with the photos already attached, then gives you a checksum so you can tell whether the file you send next month is still the file you exported today.

    It is designed for small general contractors, subcontractors and trades that do not want another per-user field platform. It does not replace a full project management system, an accounting package or legal advice.

    Questions

    Straight answers

    Do you upload my project data or photos?

    No. The form fields and photos are processed in your browser. The page has a Content-Security-Policy that blocks outbound connections, and there is no analytics or third-party script. See the privacy page for what the static host may log.

    Does the SHA-256 prove the photo was taken on a certain date?

    No. It proves that a file’s bytes match the hash recorded at export. It does not establish when the photo was taken, who took it, or whether the content is true. The PDF records the device’s local generation time, which is not an external trusted timestamp.

    What happens to GPS and other EXIF data in my photos?

    Each photo is drawn to a canvas, resized so its long edge is at most 1600 px, and encoded as a new JPEG at quality 0.85. Canvas encoding does not carry the source EXIF block, so GPS and other camera metadata are not included in the PDF. This changes the image bytes and may reduce detail; keep the originals separately.

    Can I use Chinese or other non-Latin text?

    The PDF uses the built-in Helvetica font, which covers Latin characters. Characters outside that set are shown as “?” and the tool warns you before export. Use Latin text for the PDF, or attach a translated note.

    Is this legal advice or a substitute for a contract?

    No. It is a record-organizing tool. It does not decide whether a change is valid, whether a signature is authorized, or whether a document will be accepted in a dispute. Consult a qualified professional for your situation.
